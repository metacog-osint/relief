Feed Method

Where humanitarian appeals and plans are open, how well they are funded, and where WHO is reporting outbreaks.

Each row is the publisher's own record: an IFRC Emergency Appeal or DREF allocation, a UN-coordinated plan as OCHA's Financial Tracking Service records it, a disaster ReliefWeb tracks, or a WHO Disease Outbreak News notice. An appeal or a plan shows where money was asked for, not every place in need.

Unusual for the place

Countries with IFRC appeals starting in the last 90 days, ranked by how unlikely that many would be at the country's own rate over the ten years before. The figure is the chance of at least that many appeals in 90 days at the country's past rate. Under 1% with at least 2 appeals is marked unusual (indicator RL-IND-1): a measurement against the place's own past, not a warning.

How much of each Emergency Appeal is funded

Current IFRC Emergency Appeals (marked active, end date not passed), least funded first, in Swiss francs as IFRC states them. DREF allocations are left out: they are normally funded in full when approved. IFRC keeps many appeals marked active after their end date; those are in the table below, marked, and left out here, in the totals and in the feed.

WHO outbreak notices, last 12 months

Disease and place as WHO's title gives them, with a link to WHO's notice. WHO publishes after it has verified an event, often weeks after the first report. "First" marks a country no WHO notice named in the two years before (indicator RL-IND-2): unusual for the place, not a warning.

UN-coordinated plans this year: needs and funding

OCHA's Financial Tracking Service (FTS): each plan or appeal for each country, what it requires and how much funding has been reported against it, in US dollars, largest first. People in need are the intersectoral figure in the country's own plan (OCHA's Humanitarian Programme Cycle), against last year's plan. At least 25% more people in need than last year is marked unusual (indicator RL-IND-3), a measurement against the country's own past, not a warning. Plans change scope between years, so a fall can be a narrower plan. Read through the HDX Humanitarian API; CC BY-IGO 3.0, shares and changes added by Relief.

Disasters ReliefWeb tracks now, and how much it is reporting

Disasters ReliefWeb marks ongoing or alert, with the number of reports ReliefWeb posted on each in the last 7 days against its average per week over the 12 weeks before. At least 10 reports and under a 1% chance at that average is marked unusual (indicator RL-IND-4). A count of reports measures reporting attention, not need. ReliefWeb's terms allow no republishing of its text, so only types, countries, GLIDE numbers, dates, counts and links are shown.
