Where the Red Cross and Red Crescent are asking for help, and where WHO is reporting outbreaks.

Each row is the publisher's own record: an IFRC Emergency Appeal or DREF allocation, or a WHO Disease Outbreak News notice. An IFRC appeal shows where the IFRC asked for money, not every place in need. Crises the ICRC, the UN or a government leads may have no IFRC appeal.

Unusual for the place

Countries with IFRC appeals starting in the last 90 days, ranked by how unlikely that many would be at the country's own rate over the ten years before. A measurement, not a warning: no threshold is set yet. The figure is the chance of at least that many appeals in 90 days at the country's past rate.

How much of each Emergency Appeal is funded

Current IFRC Emergency Appeals (marked active, end date not passed), least funded first, in Swiss francs as IFRC states them. DREF allocations are left out: they are normally funded in full when approved. IFRC keeps many appeals marked active after their end date; those are in the table below, marked, and left out here, in the totals and in the feed.

WHO outbreak notices, last 12 months

Disease and place as WHO's title gives them, with a link to WHO's notice. WHO publishes after it has verified an event, often weeks after the first report. "First in two years" counts WHO's notices naming that country in the two years before.
